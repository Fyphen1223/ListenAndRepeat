New TOEFL 2026: What Changed in Every Section

In January 2026, ETS introduced the most extensive redesign of the TOEFL iBT in years. If you prepared for the old test, several things you knew no longer apply. Here is what changed — and what it means for your preparation.

The headline changes

  • New scoring scale: sections are now reported on a 1–6 band scale (in 0.5 steps), aligned with the CEFR, replacing the familiar 0–30 per-section scores.
  • New task types in every section, with more emphasis on real-life communication: short announcements, emails, interviews and campus scenarios.
  • Adaptive Reading and Listening: the difficulty of later material adjusts to your performance, similar in spirit to other adaptive tests.
  • A shorter, more focused test built around practical academic and everyday English.

Section by section

SectionWhat's in the new format
ReadingShorter, more varied texts, including a "Complete the Words" task that tests vocabulary in context, alongside comprehension questions.
ListeningShort announcements, campus conversations and academic talks, including "Listen and Choose a Response" items that test how well you catch the point of a short utterance.
SpeakingTwo tasks: Listen and Repeat (repeat seven sentences from a campus-life scenario) and Take an Interview (answer questions in a simulated interview).
WritingThree tasks: Build a Sentence (arrange words into a correct sentence), Write an Email, and Write for an Academic Discussion.

What this means for your preparation

Old-format materials still build general English ability, but they do not train the new task mechanics. Listen and Repeat, for example, tests listening memory and accurate reproduction — a skill the old Speaking section never asked for. Build a Sentence tests grammar and word order directly, in seconds per item. The efficient approach is: keep building general skills, but add task-specific drills for the new formats early, so the mechanics are automatic on test day.
