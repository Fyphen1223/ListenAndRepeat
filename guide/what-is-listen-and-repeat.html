What Is the TOEFL "Listen and Repeat" Task?

Listen and Repeat is one of the two tasks in the Speaking section of the redesigned TOEFL iBT (the new format introduced in January 2026). It is exactly what the name says: you listen to sentences and repeat them back, word for word, as accurately as you can.

How the task works

You are placed in an everyday campus-life scenario — for example, taking a tour of a library or learning how to use a facility. You then hear a series of seven sentences, one at a time. After each sentence, you have a short window (roughly 8–12 seconds) to repeat it. The sentences start short and simple and become progressively longer and more complex, so the last two or three sentences are the real challenge.

What is being scored?

Your repetition is scored on how completely and accurately you reproduce the sentence: did you catch every word, keep the word order, and stay intelligible? Small slips on a short sentence are recoverable, but on the longer sentences the score depends on how much of the sentence you can hold in memory and reproduce faithfully. Pronunciation matters mainly where it affects intelligibility — this is a listening-memory and delivery task, not an accent test.

Key point: Listen and Repeat rewards a trainable skill — holding a spoken sentence in working memory and reproducing it under time pressure. It improves quickly with deliberate, repeated practice.

Why test-takers find it hard

Most learners have never practiced repeating long sentences verbatim. The common failure points are: losing the end of a long sentence, substituting a similar word for the one actually said, and freezing after a missed word instead of finishing the sentence.
